# 6. Important Grammar Notes

### Action vs Status

- **Action:** The subject performs an action.
  - *Rohan is reading a novel.*
  - *They were watching TV.*

- **Status:** The subject describes a state, condition, identity, quality, or situation.
  - *Rajat is a disciplined boy.*
  - *The mattress was comfortable.*
  - *He will be a doctor.*

### Subject Is Not the Doer

When the subject **receives the action**, the sentence is generally in **passive voice**.

**Active:**
> The workers completed the project.

**Passive:**
> The project was completed by the workers.

Here:
- **Workers** = doer
- **Project** = receiver of the action / grammatical subject in passive voice

### Since vs For

- **Since** → starting point
  - since 5 AM
  - since Monday
  - since childhood
  - since 2020

- **For** → duration
  - for two hours
  - for three days
  - for eight years

### Common Corrections

Some original English sentences have been normalized for grammar and natural English, for example:

- `Inida` → `India`
- `suger` → `sugar`
- `convert` → `concert`
- `cutted` → `cut`
- `successfull` → `successful`
- `murder hua` → `was murdered`
- `angry on me` → `angry with me`
- `in a office` → `in an office`
- `10 minuts` → `10 minutes`
- `local peoples` → `local people`

> **Learning note:** Some sentences in the source list use forms such as **"is being angry"**, **"is being reliable"**, or **"was being rich."** These are grammatically possible only in special contexts and are usually unnatural for ordinary English. In normal English, we generally say **"is angry," "is reliable,"** and **"was rich."**
